Jingwei Kang

PhD candidate · Information Retrieval Lab (IRLab), University of Amsterdam

I am a PhD candidate at the Information Retrieval Lab (IRLab) of the University of Amsterdam, supervised by Maarten de Rijke, and I work closely with Harrie Oosterhuis of Radboud University.

I study how people browse carousel interfaces in streaming and recommender systems, and how to model and evaluate that behaviour: click models, eye-tracking studies, offline metrics made for carousels, and off-policy evaluation from logged interactions. I have also worked on stochastic and online learning to rank. As a member of TAIMLab, a joint lab of Maastricht University, the University of Amsterdam and RTL, I work on unbiased and diverse video recommender systems.

Research

Most of my work starts from one observation: recommendations are often shown as carousels, yet most click models and evaluation metrics assume a single ranked list. The themes below follow from that.

How people browse carousels

Eye-tracking studies of carousel interfaces. RecGaze is the first public dataset that combines gaze, clicks and cursor movements on carousels (87 users, 3,477 interactions) and shows a golden-triangle, F-shaped browsing pattern. A follow-up tests four assumptions imported from web search and finds that most of them fail: the F-pattern holds only vertically, users follow an L-shaped path after clicking, the examination hypothesis does not hold, and users skip carousel headings.

PapersRecGaze (SIGIR ’25) · Following the Eye-Tracking Evidence (SIGIR ’26)

Click models for carousels

Click-model taxonomies were built for single ranked lists. I replace them with three mathematically grounded design choices that cover single-list, grid and carousel click models, both probabilistic graphical models and neural ones, and use those choices to derive a click model made for carousels.

PapersRethinking Click Models (ICTIR ’25) · PhD research overview (RecSys ’26)

Evaluating carousel recommendation

Offline metrics and off-policy evaluation for carousel layouts. N2DCG, the usual NDCG adaptation for carousels, relies on an ideal ranking that violates carousel constraints and on a discount that does not match observed browsing; our reformulation grounds both in eye-tracking data. Off-policy evaluation of carousel recommendation policies from logged data belongs to the same programme.

PapersRevisiting N2DCG (CIKM ’26) · PhD research overview (RecSys ’26)

Learning to rank

Stochastic and online learning to rank. Estimating the Hessian of ranking objectives makes gradient-boosted decision trees usable for stochastic learning to rank; with collaborators I also built a large-scale web search dataset for federated online learning to rank.

PapersEstimating the Hessian Matrix of Ranking Objectives (SIGIR ’24) · A Large-Scale Web Search Dataset (CIKM ’25)
