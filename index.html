Jingwei Kang

PhD candidate · Information Retrieval Lab (IRLab), University of Amsterdam

I study recommender systems in streaming media services, where recommendations are shown in carousel interfaces. My work focuses on modelling user behaviour and on offline and off-policy evaluation; I have also worked on learning to rank. I am a PhD candidate at the University of Amsterdam, supervised by Maarten de Rijke and Harrie Oosterhuis.

Research

Most of my work starts from one observation: recommendations are often shown as carousels, yet most click models and evaluation metrics assume a single ranked list. The themes below follow from that.

How people browse carousels

RecGaze is the first public dataset that combines eye tracking, clicks and cursor movements on carousels (87 users, 3,477 interactions) and shows a golden-triangle, F-shaped browsing pattern. A second study tests four assumptions imported from web search and finds that most of them fail: the F-pattern holds only vertically, users follow an L-shaped path after clicking, the examination hypothesis does not hold, and users skip carousel headings.

PapersRecGaze (SIGIR ’25) · Following the Eye-Tracking Evidence (SIGIR ’26)

Click models for carousels

Click-model taxonomies were built for single ranked lists. I replace them with three mathematically grounded design choices that cover single-list, grid and carousel click models, both probabilistic graphical models and neural ones, and use those choices to derive a click model made for carousels.

PapersRethinking Click Models (ICTIR ’25) · PhD research overview (RecSys ’26)

Evaluating carousels offline and off-policy

N2DCG, the usual NDCG adaptation for carousels, relies on an ideal ranking that violates carousel constraints and on a discount that does not match observed browsing; our reformulation grounds both in eye-tracking data. Off-policy evaluation of carousel recommendation policies from logged data belongs to the same programme.

PapersRevisiting N2DCG (CIKM ’26) · PhD research overview (RecSys ’26)

Stochastic and online learning to rank

Estimating the Hessian of ranking objectives makes gradient-boosted decision trees usable for stochastic learning to rank; with collaborators I also built a large-scale web search dataset for federated online learning to rank.

PapersEstimating the Hessian Matrix of Ranking Objectives (SIGIR ’24) · A Large-Scale Web Search Dataset (CIKM ’25)
