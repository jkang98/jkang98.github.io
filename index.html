Jingwei Kang

PhD candidate · Information Retrieval Lab (IRLab) · University of Amsterdam

I am a PhD candidate at the Information Retrieval Lab of the University of Amsterdam, supervised by Maarten de Rijke. I study how people browse carousel interfaces in streaming and recommendation services, and how to model and evaluate that behaviour: click models, eye-tracking studies, carousel-specific evaluation metrics, and offline and off-policy evaluation.

I also work on online and stochastic learning to rank, and I collaborate closely with Harrie Oosterhuis of Radboud University. I am a member of TAIMLab, the Trustworthy AI in Media Lab run by Maastricht University, the University of Amsterdam and the media company RTL, where I work on unbiased and diverse video recommender systems.

Research

How people browse carousels

Streaming and recommendation services lay out their catalogues as carousels: rows of items that scroll sideways, stacked down the page. With eye tracking we record how people actually look at them. The RecGaze dataset makes such recordings public for the first time, and testing established web-search assumptions against carousel eye-tracking data shows that most of them hold only in part, or fail outright, once the interface is a carousel.

Papers RecGaze (SIGIR ’25) · Following the Eye-Tracking Evidence (SIGIR ’26)

Click models for carousel interfaces

Click models explain how clicks arise from what people see and what they prefer. Instead of sorting them into older taxonomies, I categorise click models by three mathematically grounded design choices. They cover single-list, grid and carousel interfaces, and probabilistic graphical models and neural models alike. From them I derive a new click model for carousels. My PhD programme extends this into a click-model design framework built on the relations between observed variables, together with discrete-choice click models.

Papers Rethinking Click Models (ICTIR ’25) · From Click Modeling to Offline and Off-Policy Evaluation (RecSys ’26)

Evaluating carousel recommendation

N2DCG, the NDCG adaptation for carousels, rests on an ideal ranking and a browsing discount that the eye-tracking evidence does not support, so I reformulate it on empirical grounds. The same line of work leads to carousel-specific offline metrics and to the off-policy evaluation of carousel recommendation policies from logged interaction data.

Papers Revisiting N2DCG (CIKM ’26) · From Click Modeling to Offline and Off-Policy Evaluation (RecSys ’26)

Learning to rank

Beyond carousels, I work on learning to rank. Estimating the Hessian of ranking objectives makes gradient-boosted decision trees usable for stochastic learning to rank, and a large-scale web-search dataset we built supports research on federated online learning to rank.

Papers Estimating the Hessian Matrix of Ranking Objectives (SIGIR ’24) · A Large-Scale Web Search Dataset for Federated Online Learning to Rank (CIKM ’25)
