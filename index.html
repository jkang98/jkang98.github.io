Jingwei Kang

PhD candidate · Information Retrieval Lab (IRLab), University of Amsterdam

I study recommender systems in streaming media services, where recommendations are shown in carousel interfaces. My work focuses on user behaviour modelling and offline evaluation; I have also worked on learning to rank. I am a PhD candidate at the University of Amsterdam, supervised by prof. dr. Maarten de Rijke and dr. Harrie Oosterhuis.

  1. Revisiting N2DCG: An Empirically Grounded Reformulation of Carousel Recommendation Evaluation

    Jingwei Kang (first author), Santiago de Leon-Martinez, Maarten de Rijke, Harrie Oosterhuis

    CIKM ’26 · 35th ACM International Conference on Information and Knowledge Management, Rome, Italy, November 2026 · to appear

    Shows that N2DCG, the NDCG adaptation for carousels, uses an ideal ranking that violates carousel constraints and a discount that does not match observed browsing; proposes a reformulated metric grounded in eye-tracking data.

  3. Following the Eye-Tracking Evidence: Established Web-Search Assumptions Fail in Carousel Interfaces

    Jingwei Kang (first author), Maarten de Rijke, Harrie Oosterhuis

    SIGIR ’26 · 49th International ACM SIGIR Conference on Research and Development in Information Retrieval, Melbourne, Australia, July 2026, pp. 734–744

    Tests four web-search behavioural assumptions on carousel eye-tracking data: the F-pattern holds only vertically, users follow an L-shaped pattern after clicking, the examination hypothesis fails, and users skip carousel headings.

  2. Rethinking Click Models in Light of Carousel Interfaces: Theory-Based Categorization and Design of Click Models

    Jingwei Kang (first author), Maarten de Rijke, Santiago de Leon-Martinez, Harrie Oosterhuis

    ICTIR ’25 · International ACM SIGIR Conference on Innovative Concepts and Theories in Information Retrieval, July 2025, pp. 44–55

    Replaces outdated click-model taxonomies with three mathematically grounded design choices that cover single-list, grid and carousel click models (PGMs and neural), and derives a new carousel click model from them.

  3. RecGaze: The First Eye Tracking and User Interaction Dataset for Carousel Interfaces
